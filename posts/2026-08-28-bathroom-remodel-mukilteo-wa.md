## Neighborhood context

Some homes sit on slopes with tricky material access and limited staging. Upstairs baths over living rooms need leak-conscious detailing and a written plan for temporary living if the only full bath is offline. Owners often combine bath updates with bedroom refreshes for aging-in-place or resale readiness — keep those scopes on one schedule so waterproofing, electrical, and finishes do not fight each other.

Ferry-adjacent and bluff streets can constrain dumpster placement and delivery windows. Tell bidders about access constraints up front so proposals reflect real logistics, not a flat-lot assumption imported from inland Snohomish County.

## Climate, site, and permits

Prioritize continuous waterproofing, outdoor exhaust, and condensation control on exterior walls. Fixture moves typically need plumbing permits; new circuits need electrical permits. Confirm whether the [City of Mukilteo](https://mukilteowa.gov/) processes your building and trade permits for the parcel, and build review time into the schedule rather than treating inspection holds as a surprise.

Cool walls facing the Sound argue for honest insulation and vapor details when a window sits in or beside the wet room. If you are replacing that window during the bath remodel, integrate flashing and trim so the waterproofing stack and the exterior opening read as one assembly.

## Design, process, and coordination

Choose wet-area systems over shortcuts. Slip-resistant flooring helps with sandy beach-day traffic. Quiet fans with humidity sensors suit primary baths. Glass measurements should follow final curb and valve realities, not optimistic drawings alone.

Pressure-balanced or thermostatic valves should be accessible via a panel or removable escutcheon strategy your tile design anticipates. Moving a toilet even slightly can force drain work that dominates the budget — lock the plumbing plan before tile procurement.

## FAQ

**Does a view bath need special glass?**  
Privacy films, shades, and thoughtful window placement matter as much as shower glass. Coordinate early so flashing and waterproofing are coherent.

**Can I keep a clawfoot tub?**  
Yes with proper floor structure, supply stops, and splash-zone honesty — not as an afterthought over a weak substrate.

**How do I compare Mukilteo bids?**  
Align membrane specs, fan termination, permit ownership, valve access strategy, and exclusions line by line — not just the bottom line.

## Bluff and hillside detailing notes

Mukilteo baths on exterior walls facing wind need careful flashing at any window in the wet room, and honest insulation details to reduce condensation. Sand and beach gear argue for flooring that tolerates grit and rinse-off habits near entries — even if the bath is upstairs, habits travel. Specify shower glass hardware you are willing to wipe down in a marine-influenced environment.

Toilet paper holder blocking, robe hook backing, shower door towel clips, and exhaust grille locations should appear on elevations. These small misses create a finished bath that still feels incomplete.
