## Local context

Some homes sit on slopes with tricky material access. Upstairs baths over living rooms need leak-conscious detailing. Owners often combine bath updates with bedroom refreshes for aging-in-place or resale readiness.

## Climate and permitting

Prioritize continuous waterproofing, outdoor exhaust, and condensation control on exterior walls. Fixture moves typically need plumbing permits; new circuits need electrical permits. Know whether City of Mukilteo processes your permits and build review time into the schedule.

## Materials and process

Choose wet-area systems over shortcuts. Slip-resistant flooring helps with sandy beach-day traffic. Quiet fans with humidity sensors suit primary baths. Glass measurements should follow final curb and valve realities, not optimistic drawings alone.

## Bluff and hillside detailing notes

Mukilteo baths on exterior walls facing wind need careful flashing at any window in the wet room, and honest insulation details to reduce condensation. If you are replacing a window during the bath remodel, integrate that scope so waterproofing and trim are coherent.

Sand and beach gear argue for flooring that tolerates grit and rinse-off habits near entries — even if the bath is upstairs, habits travel. Specify shower glass hardware you are willing to wipe down in a marine-influenced environment.

## Valve and drain choices with longevity in mind

Pressure-balanced or thermostatic valves should be accessible via a panel or removable escutcheon strategy your tile design anticipates. Moving a toilet even slightly can force drain work that dominates the budget — lock the plumbing plan before tile procurement.

## Punch-list items people forget

Toilet paper holder blocking, robe hook backing, shower door towel clips, and exhaust grille locations should appear on elevations. These small misses create a finished bath that still feels incomplete.
