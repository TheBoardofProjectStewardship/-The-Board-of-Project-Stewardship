## Local context

## Technical coordination

- Confirm panel capacity and dedicated circuit needs
- Coordinate mat or cable layout with drain location and niched walls
- Maintain waterproofing continuity above or as specified by the system
- Account for finished height against adjacent flooring
- Place thermostats where users can reach them without moisture abuse

Permits: electrical work for floor heat typically requires permitted electrical scope — include it explicitly.

## FAQ

**Electric vs hydronic in a single bath?**  
Electric mats are common for single-room bath upgrades; hydronic is more whole-house territory.

**Will heated floors fix a cold room alone?**  
They help surfaces; you may still need proper HVAC/ventilation design for air temperature and moisture.

**Can I add heat without replacing tile?**  
Usually no for electric mats — they belong under the finish assembly.

## Comfort benefits and honest limitations

Warm tile changes how an Edmonds bath feels in January, and it can help surfaces dry after showers. It will not replace a proper HVAC plan for the suite, and it will not fix a shower that ponds. Treat heat as a layer in a moisture-managed room.

Discuss thermostat programming so floors are warm when you wake without running at peak unnecessarily. Some households zone the shower area differently from the vanity zone depending on mat layout rules — follow manufacturer spacing and keep mats away from toilet flanges as directed.

## Failure modes to avoid

- Installing heat over an uncorrected soft subfloor
- Piercing mats during later fixture installs
- Skipping electrical permits
- Choosing finishes incompatible with system temperature guidance

Require as-built photos of mat layout before thinset. That documentation is invaluable if a tile needs repair years later.
