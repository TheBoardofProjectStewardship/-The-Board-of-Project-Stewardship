## Key Takeaways

- **Most Mill Creek deck replacements need a City building permit.** The City's [Do I Need a Permit? flyer](https://millcreekwa.gov/sites/default/files/2025-08/do-i-need-a-permit-flyer-updated-2025-08-27.pdf) lists decks more than 30 inches above grade — including repairs — as permit work, and the exemption in [Mill Creek Municipal Code 15.04](https://ecode360.com/50644397) covers only decks that are not more than 30 inches above grade at any point, not attached to the house, and not serving the required exit door.
- **Apply through OpenGov, and plan to the City's design criteria.** Mill Creek takes building permit applications through its [OpenGov portal](https://millcreekwa.portal.opengov.com/), and its [Building Codes and Guidance](https://millcreekwa.gov/topics/building-codes-and-guidance) page publishes the snow load, seismic category, wind speed and 12-inch minimum frost depth your designer should use.
- **Deck lighting is an L&I electrical permit, and every firm gets checked.** The City notes electrical permits are administered by Washington L&I, so low-voltage or line-voltage deck lighting goes through [L&I electrical permits](https://lni.wa.gov/licensing-permits/electrical/electrical-permits-fees-and-inspections/) — and every contractor should be looked up on [Washington L&I Verify](https://secure.lni.wa.gov/verify/) before you sign.

## Why winter is the right time to plan a Mill Creek deck replacement

Mill Creek is a south Snohomish County city of suburban neighborhoods where many back decks look out onto wet firs and cedars for much of the year. That setting is exactly why so many decks here reach the end of their life at the same time: boards stay damp from October through spring, joist tops hold water under the decking, and the ledger connection at the house rim takes the brunt of every roof drip and splashback.

Winter is when the problems show — soft boards, mossy surfaces, a bounce near the house — and it is also the best time to plan. Design, permit review, contractor selection and material ordering can all happen while it rains, so tear-out can start when the weather turns. This guide stays on what the City of Mill Creek and Washington State actually publish, plus the construction details that decide whether a replacement deck lasts.

For the county-wide picture, see the Board of Project Stewardship's earlier [Snohomish County deck replacement guide](../posts/2026-10-10-deck-replacement-snohomish-county-wa.html). For local firms, contacts and other Mill Creek guides, start with the Board's [Mill Creek directory](../mill-creek.html) and the [permits overview](../permits.html).

## When a Mill Creek deck needs a permit

Mill Creek amends the International Residential Code's list of permit-exempt work in [MCMC 15.04.055](https://ecode360.com/50644397). For decks, the exemption is narrow. A deck is exempt only when all three of these are true:

1. It is not more than 30 inches above grade **at any point**.
2. It is **not attached** to a dwelling.
3. It does **not serve the exit door** required by Section R311.4 of the residential code.

Most back-of-house decks in Mill Creek fail at least one of those tests. They bolt to the house rim, they sit outside a slider or French door, and on a sloping lot one corner is often well above 30 inches even when the end by the door looks low.

The City's own [Do I Need a Permit? flyer](https://millcreekwa.gov/sites/default/files/2025-08/do-i-need-a-permit-flyer-updated-2025-08-27.pdf) puts it more plainly for homeowners: "Decks more than 30" above grade – including repairs" are on the permits-required list. The same flyer opens with the general rule that no building, electrical, mechanical or plumbing system may be installed, constructed, altered, repaired, replaced or demolished without a permit, and it points questions to the Building Department at 425-551-7254. Treat a full tear-out and rebuild as permit work unless the City tells you otherwise for your specific deck.

The flyer is a guide, not the code itself — the City says the information may not be complete and that additional permits may apply. If you are unsure, call the Permit Counter before you sign a contract, not after demolition.

## How Mill Creek wants you to apply

What the City publishes about the process:

- **OpenGov for building permits.** The [Permits and Licensing](https://millcreekwa.gov/permits) page says the City uses OpenGov for the application and approval process for building, mechanical and plumbing permits, with a direct link to [apply for a building permit](https://millcreekwa.portal.opengov.com/categories/1084/record-types/6492). You may need to set up an account.
- **Status in the same place.** The [Building Codes and Guidance](https://millcreekwa.gov/topics/building-codes-and-guidance) page links OpenGov for both applying and checking the status of a building permit.
- **The Permit Counter.** City Hall North is at 15720 Main Street, Suite 110. The Permit Counter phone is 425-551-7254, and the permits page lists walk-in and appointment-only hours, plus pre-application consultations with staff.
- **Electrical is separate.** Mill Creek's codes page states that electrical permits are administered by the Washington State Department of Labor & Industries. Mill Creek does not appear on L&I's [list of cities that issue their own electrical permits](https://lni.wa.gov/licensing-permits/electrical/electrical-permits-fees-and-inspections/city-electrical-permits-inspections), so plan on L&I for any deck lighting or receptacles.

The Board does not publish fee tables or review timelines here. Mill Creek's code says permit fees are set by City Council resolution and that permit valuation includes all labor, materials, overhead and profit — ask the City what applies to your project.

## Mill Creek's design numbers

The City's [Building Codes and Guidance](https://millcreekwa.gov/topics/building-codes-and-guidance) page lists the codes adopted as amended by the State Building Code Council — including the 2021 International Residential Code — and publishes design criteria for Mill Creek:

- Ground snow load: 25 psf
- Seismic design category: D1
- Wind speed: 110 mph, exposure B and C
- Minimum frost depth: 12 inches

Those numbers feed directly into deck design. Snow load drives joist, beam and footing sizing in the code tables; the frost depth sets how deep footings must go; and seismic and wind categories are part of why lateral connections at the house matter. A bid that does not mention footing depth or how the deck is tied back to the house is not finished.

## Washington deck code basics: ledger, footings, lateral load

Washington's amendments to the residential code live in [WAC 51-51](https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=51-51), and the deck section is [WAC 51-51-0507 (Section R507 — Decks)](https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=51-51-0507). It is a long, table-heavy section. Homeowners do not need to memorize it, but a few items are worth knowing by name so you can read a bid:

- **Ledger flashing.** The ledger table notes say ledgers shall be flashed in accordance with Section R703.4 "to prevent water from contacting the house band joist." On a Mill Creek replacement, this is where hidden rot usually lives. Expect siding to come off at the ledger line, the rim to be inspected, and new flashing and membrane installed before the new ledger goes on.
- **Band joist.** R507 specifies minimum band joist material where a ledger attaches, and that band joists bear fully on the primary structure. If the existing rim is rotted, it has to be repaired before the deck can hang from it.
- **Footings.** Footing size tables tie tributary area, load and soil bearing value together. Combined with the City's 12-inch minimum frost depth, that sets what the footings look like.
- **Posts.** Post size and maximum height are tabulated by species and tributary area.
- **Lateral load.** Section R507.9.2 requires lateral loads to be transferred to the ground or to a structure capable of carrying them; where the prescriptive detail is used, hold-down tension devices go in at least two locations per deck, within 24 inches of each end.

Guards, stairs and handrails are also part of the residential code review. Ask your designer to show the guard and stair details on the plan set rather than leaving them to the field.

## PNW rot and moisture details worth writing into the contract

Mill Creek's climate punishes shortcuts. These are the details that separate a deck that lasts from one that is back in the same condition in a decade:

- **Open up the rim.** On a replacement, the ledger and house rim are inspected once the old deck is off. Write how rot discovered there will be handled — and priced — before demolition, so a discovery does not become a dispute.
- **Flashing and membrane at the ledger.** Name the flashing and membrane products in the scope, and require that the weather-resistive barrier laps over the top flashing.
- **Joist protection.** Joist-top tape is common under composite boards in wet climates. Ask whether it is included.
- **Footings and post bases.** Footing depth to the City's minimum, posts held off concrete on manufactured bases, and hardware rated for treated lumber.
- **Drainage and clearance.** Grading that moves water away from footings, and enough clearance under low decks for air to move.
- **Fasteners.** Corrosion-resistant fasteners and hangers matched to treated lumber, installed with every specified nail or screw.

## Deck lighting and outlets: L&I, not the City

Post-cap lights, stair lights and an outdoor receptacle are common add-ons. In Mill Creek, the City's codes page says electrical permits are administered by Washington L&I, so the electrical permit and inspection come from [L&I](https://lni.wa.gov/licensing-permits/electrical/electrical-permits-fees-and-inspections/), separate from the City building permit. Ask each bidder who pulls the electrical permit and whether the electrical work is done by a licensed electrical contractor. Keep that permit number with your house papers alongside the building permit.

## How to shortlist and verify the deck crew

Washington L&I's [hiring a contractor guidance](https://lni.wa.gov/licensing-permits/contractors/hiring-a-contractor/) recommends confirming active registration, bonding and insurance, getting multiple written bids, and using a written contract. Look up every firm — the deck builder and any electrician — on [L&I Verify](https://secure.lni.wa.gov/verify/). Ask each bidder for recent Snohomish County deck replacements, photos of the ledger and flashing stage (not only finished decks), and a written scope that states:

- Whether the deck is attached, how high it sits above grade at its highest point, and therefore whether the City permit applies.
- Who applies on OpenGov, who schedules inspections, and who pulls any L&I electrical permit.
- The ledger attachment method, flashing and membrane products, and how rim rot will be handled if found.
- Footing size and depth, post bases, hangers and the lateral connection detail.
- Decking, guard and stair materials by name, plus haul-off of the old deck.

Walk through the Board's [verify a contractor](../verify-contractor.html) checklist, [hiring a contractor](../hiring-a-contractor.html), [bonds and insurance](../bonds-and-insurance.html), and [red flags](../red-flags-hiring.html) pages before you sign. A bidder who says an attached Mill Creek deck "doesn't need a permit" has not read the City's own flyer.

## Next steps for a Mill Creek deck replacement

1. Measure the deck's highest point above grade and note whether it attaches to the house or serves an exit door — then read the City's [permit flyer](https://millcreekwa.gov/sites/default/files/2025-08/do-i-need-a-permit-flyer-updated-2025-08-27.pdf).
2. Call the Permit Counter at 425-551-7254 or book a pre-application consultation if the answer is not clear.
3. Have a designer or builder prepare plans using Mill Creek's published design criteria and Washington's R507 deck provisions.
4. Get at least three written bids that name the ledger flashing, footings, lateral connection and materials; verify every firm with L&I.
5. Apply through [OpenGov](https://millcreekwa.portal.opengov.com/) over the winter, add an L&I electrical permit if you want lighting, and schedule tear-out for drier spring weather.

A Mill Creek deck replacement worth having is one that is permitted, flashed at the house, set on proper footings and tied back against lateral load. Start with the City's permit flyer, read the bids against the code, and hire a crew that puts those details in writing.
