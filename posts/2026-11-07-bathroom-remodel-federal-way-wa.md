## Key Takeaways

- **Start at the City of Federal Way Permit Center.** Residential electrical, mechanical, and plumbing permits apply online through [MyBuildingPermit](https://mybuildingpermit.com/); building work and plan review go through the City's [Permit Center](https://www.federalwaywa.gov/page/permit-center) and electronic document submittal path when plans are required.
- **Finish-only work and a wet-wall rebuild are different tickets.** Federal Way's [residential permit exemptions](https://www.federalwaywa.gov/page/residential-permit-exemptions) list painting, papering, tiling, carpeting, cabinets, and countertops as finish work that often skips a building permit — but opening walls, relocating drains or valves, and replacing a tub or shower are not "paint and tile." Confirm your scope with the Permit Center at 253-835-2607.
- **Federal Way issues its own electrical permits.** Washington L&I's [city electrical permits and inspections list](https://lni.wa.gov/licensing-permits/electrical/electrical-permits-fees-and-inspections/city-electrical-permits-inspections) includes the Federal Way Permit Center, so bathroom circuits, GFCI upgrades, fans, and heated-floor circuits go to the City — then re-check every firm on [Washington L&I Verify](https://secure.lni.wa.gov/verify/) before you sign.

## Three common scopes, three different permit answers

Sort the project before you call anyone:

1. **Cosmetic refresh.** Paint, new tile over a sound, already-waterproofed substrate, a replacement vanity and countertop in the same location, new mirrors and accessories. Under the City's finish-work language on the [residential permit exemptions](https://www.federalwaywa.gov/page/residential-permit-exemptions) page — painting, papering, tiling, carpeting, cabinets, countertops and similar finish work — this often needs no building permit. The moment you modify plumbing lines, replace a tub or shower, or add circuits, that answer changes.
2. **Fixture and systems update in the same layout.** New valves, a shower pan rebuilt from the studs, relocated drains, a new exhaust fan duct, new lighting or GFCI circuits. Expect plumbing and electrical permits at minimum, and ask the Permit Center whether a building permit is also required.
3. **Layout change or expansion.** Moving walls, enlarging the room, converting a tub to a curbless shower that changes framing, or borrowing space from a closet or bedroom. This is building-permit territory. If it touches structure or a critical area, plan for a fuller review.

Write that classification into the first line of every bid you request. It keeps contractors comparing the same job.

## How Federal Way wants you to apply

What the City publishes about the process:

- **Trade permits online.** The [Permit Center](https://www.federalwaywa.gov/page/permit-center) says residential electrical, mechanical, and plumbing permits are applied for through [MyBuildingPermit](https://mybuildingpermit.com/). The older [E-Permits archive](https://epermits.cityoffederalway.com/PublicPortal/FederalWayDev/common/index.jsp) is for looking up past permits, not for new submittals.
- **Building plans when required.** For work that needs plan review, the City points owners and contractors to its [electronic document submittal](https://www.cityoffederalway.com/page/electronic-document-submittal) path and to Building Permits handouts for Building, Electrical, Mechanical, Plumbing, Demolition, and Fire Prevention.
- **Know the counter.** Staff answer at permitcenter@federalwaywa.gov and 253-835-2607. The exemptions page itself says: if in doubt, call the Permit Center.
- **Inspections.** The City's [inspection information](https://www.federalwaywa.gov/page/inspection-information) page directs scheduling through [inspection.mybuildingpermit.com](https://inspection.mybuildingpermit.com/). A specific clock time is not guaranteed; change or cancel same-day inspections through the Permit Center.
- **Watch for payment scams.** The Permit Center carries a fraud alert: the City will not unexpectedly ask for permit payment by wire transfer based solely on an email.

Do not invent fee tables or review timelines from memory. Ask the Permit Center what applies to your address and scope.

## What the residential exemptions actually cover — and what they do not

Federal Way's [residential permit exemptions](https://www.federalwaywa.gov/page/residential-permit-exemptions) page tracks International Residential Code Section R105.2-style lists for building, electrical, mechanical, and plumbing. The finish-work line that homeowners care about most is painting, papering, tiling, carpeting, cabinets, countertops and similar finish work. Plumbing exemptions cover stopping leaks in drains, water, soil, waste, or vent pipe, and clearing stoppages or repairing leaks in pipes, valves, or fixtures, plus removing and reinstalling water closets — **provided** the work does not require replacement or rearrangement of valves, pipes, or fixtures. Electrical exemptions include reinstallation of attachment-plug receptacles (not the outlets themselves) and replacement of branch-circuit overcurrent devices of the required capacity in the same location.

Two cautions sit on the same page:

- **Critical areas are not exempt.** Any work in a critical area — wetlands, streams, landslide hazard areas and their buffers — is not exempt from permits.
- **Separate permits may still apply.** The City notes that unless otherwise exempted, separate permits may be required for listed items. Finish work without a building permit is not a free pass to skip plumbing or electrical tickets when those systems change.

Regional [MyBuildingPermit Tip Sheet 00](https://www.mybuildingpermit.com/sites/default/files/TS-00_Permits%20Required%20and%20Not%20Required_FINAL_6.11.26.pdf) draws a related line many Federal Way crews already know: bathroom and kitchen fixture replacements without plumbing-line modifications (sinks and toilets) are commonly listed among examples where a building permit is not required, while **tub and shower replacement does require a permit**. Federal Way is not a named column jurisdiction on that sheet — confirm locally before you treat the tip sheet as the City's final answer.

## Electrical: City permit, not L&I

Across most of Washington, electrical permits come from L&I. Federal Way is one of the exceptions. L&I's [City Electrical Permits & Inspections](https://lni.wa.gov/licensing-permits/electrical/electrical-permits-fees-and-inspections/city-electrical-permits-inspections) page lists the Federal Way Permit Center at 33325 8th Ave South, phone 253-835-2607, among cities that issue permits and perform inspections inside city limits. L&I does not issue electrical permits or do electrical inspections in those cities.

That matters for a bathroom remodel. New vanity lighting, GFCI-protected receptacles, an upgraded exhaust fan circuit, a heated-floor circuit, or a panel space check all sit on the City path — the same path described in the Board's [Federal Way electrical panel](../posts/2026-10-20-electrical-panel-upgrade-federal-way-wa.html) guide for larger service work. L&I's page also links the Department of Revenue [address lookup](https://webgis.dor.wa.gov/taxratelookup/SalesTax.aspx) for checking whether an address is inside a city. A Federal Way mailing address alone does not prove the parcel is inside city limits near the edge of town.

Put the split in the contract: building (when required) and plumbing tickets, plus a City electrical permit for the circuits in the scope. The electrician who does the work should buy the electrical permit, and the general contractor should show permit numbers on the job board.

## Wet walls, waterproofing and details worth writing into the scope

Permits get a bathroom inspected. The scope decides whether it lasts. In Federal Way homes — many with older pan liners, mixed plumbing eras, and exhaust fans that were never ducted outdoors — the recurring failures are water behind tile, under-ventilated rooms, and patchwork plumbing hidden behind new finishes. Put these in writing before demolition:

- **Waterproofing system named, not implied.** "Waterproof shower" is not a spec. Name the membrane or board system, how seams, corners, niches, and the curb or curbless transition are treated, and whether a flood test is done before tile. The Board's [bathroom waterproofing guide](../bathroom-waterproofing-guide.html) covers the questions to ask.
- **Drain type and slope.** Center drain or linear drain, where it sits, and how the floor is sloped. Curbless showers often require framing or subfloor changes, which can move a project out of the cosmetic category.
- **Ventilation.** An exhaust fan ducted to the outdoors, with the duct route and termination shown. Fans that dump into an attic or joist bay are a common inspection and moisture problem.
- **Valves and supply lines.** If walls are open, decide whether to replace older supply piping and shutoffs now. Rearranging valves and pipes is outside the plumbing exemption language on the City's page.
- **Electrical layout.** GFCI protection, vanity and shower lighting, a heated-floor circuit if planned, and panel capacity — on the City electrical path.
- **Stay-in-home sequencing.** If this is the only full bath, write where showers and toilets move during demolition and rough-in so the household is not improvising around an open wet wall.

## How to shortlist and verify the remodel team

Washington L&I's [hiring a contractor guidance](https://lni.wa.gov/licensing-permits/contractors/hiring-a-contractor/) recommends confirming active registration, bonding and insurance, collecting multiple written bids, and using a written contract. Look up every firm — general contractor, electrician, plumber — on [L&I Verify](https://secure.lni.wa.gov/verify/). Ask each bidder for recent south King County bathroom projects, photos of rough-in and waterproofing stages (not only finished rooms), and a written scope that states:

- Which of the three scope categories the job falls into, and whether a building permit is expected in addition to plumbing and electrical.
- Who pulls each City permit on [MyBuildingPermit](https://mybuildingpermit.com/) and who schedules inspections at [inspection.mybuildingpermit.com](https://inspection.mybuildingpermit.com/).
- The named waterproofing system, drain type, fan model and duct route, and whether a flood test is part of the schedule.
- How temporary bath function is handled if you stay in the house.
- How hazardous-materials testing is handled in an older house before tear-out.

Walk through the Board's [verify a contractor](../verify-contractor.html) checklist, [hiring a contractor](../hiring-a-contractor.html), [bonds and insurance](../bonds-and-insurance.html), and [red flags](../red-flags-hiring.html) pages before you sign. A bidder who assumes Federal Way electrical is a statewide L&I permit has not checked the City's own path.

## Next steps for a Federal Way bathroom remodel

1. Confirm the parcel is inside Federal Way city limits, then open the [Permit Center](https://www.federalwaywa.gov/page/permit-center) materials and [MyBuildingPermit](https://mybuildingpermit.com/).
2. Sort the scope into finish-only, fixture/systems update, or layout change — and write that sort into every bid request.
3. Plan City electrical permits for bathroom circuits; do not buy an L&I electrical permit inside Federal Way city limits.
4. Name the waterproofing system, drain, fan duct route and inspection holds in the contract before demolition.
5. Get at least three written bids, verify every firm with L&I, and keep plan sets and permit numbers with your house papers.

A Federal Way bathroom remodel worth living with is one that matches the City's permit map, opens wet walls only once, passes plumbing and electrical inspections in order, and keeps the contract honest about what is finish work and what is construction. Start with the Permit Center, the exemptions page, and a crew that puts those details in writing.
