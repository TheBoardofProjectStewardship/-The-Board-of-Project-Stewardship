Project showcase · Magnolia, Seattle

Magnolia house, basement ADU, and one-car garage

2360 Magnolia Blvd W, Seattle, WA 98199

Three permit sets on one Seattle lot: a main-level remodel with a new roof, a new accessory dwelling unit in the existing basement, and a detached one-car garage.

House
One story over a daylight basement. Main level 3,117 SF.
Main level
Roof redesign due to existing roof leakage. Main bedroom and bath expanded; living room, kitchen, and dining rearranged; two bedrooms and the laundry moved; 27 SF of the deck added to dining.
ADU
New 865 SF unit in the existing basement: living, bedroom, kitchen, bathroom, and laundry. Its own entry landing.
Garage
Detached one-car garage, 225 SF inside (12'-0" by 20'-0"), proposed height 12'-0", metal roof.
Lot
9,350 SF (85.00 by 110.0 feet), zoned NR3, City of Seattle. Between Magnolia Blvd W and Constance Dr W; the basement daylights on the Magnolia Blvd W side.
Roof
Standing seam metal on the house and on the garage.

Build progress

Roof. The house gets one new low-slope roof of standing seam metal. It falls toward a gutter on the street side and overhangs the walls. The garage has its own metal roof. The plans call it a roof redesign because the old roof leaked.

Porch. The existing 60 SF open porch sits at the entry on the street side. The ADU gets a small new landing of its own at the basement level.

Deck. The existing deck runs along the main level on the downhill side, with a stair to grade. The plans replace its railings with glass railings and fold 27 SF of it into the dining room.

ADU. The new 865 SF accessory dwelling unit fills part of the existing basement, on the side where the lot falls away and the basement wall is out of the ground. It has a living room, bedroom, kitchen, bathroom, and laundry, and its own door.

Garage. A detached one-car garage, 12 by 20 feet inside, stands near the street with its own slab and a single overhead door. The plans give it a 12-foot height.

Foundation. The basement walls carry the house down to the footings. On the uphill side they hold back soil; on the downhill side they rise out of the ground with doors and windows.

Partitions. The inside walls divide the rooms. On a real house, check what sits on a wall before anyone takes it out. The remodel moves several of them.

Framing. Studs stand between a bottom plate and a top plate. That skeleton is the frame. Siding and the glass go on it later.

Headers. The tall windows and doors on the deck side take studs out of the wall. A header, a short beam across each opening, carries that load to the studs on both sides. This sketch does not size them.

Footings. The house and the garage send weight down their walls into the footings. Those footings have to suit that load. This sketch does not size them.

Structure. The main level sits on the basement walls: a floor, walls, and the roof above. A new roof has to tie into the frame already there.

Stairs. The existing stair runs from the main level down to the basement. A stair needs a clear path, headroom, and a rail.

Teaching sketch. Not a structural design.
