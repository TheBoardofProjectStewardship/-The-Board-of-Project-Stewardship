## Key Takeaways

- **A water heater swap in Marysville is permitted work.** The City's adopted plumbing code ([Marysville Municipal Code Chapter 16.08](https://ecode360.com/49264480)) covers "water heating and treating equipment," and its plumbing permit fee table lists a line item "for each water heater and/or vent." Applications go through the City's online [Permit Portal](https://permits.marysvillewa.gov/).
- **Electrical permits are local here, too.** Washington L&I's list of [cities that run their own electrical permits and inspections](https://lni.wa.gov/licensing-permits/electrical/electrical-permits-fees-and-inspections/city-electrical-permits-inspections) includes Marysville, and the City's [Building Services](https://www.marysvillewa.gov/97/Building-services) page notes its electrical permit processing. That matters if you are moving to a heat pump water heater or adding a circuit.
- **Paid plumbing work needs a certified plumber.** L&I's [plumber program](https://lni.wa.gov/licensing-permits/plumbing/about-the-plumber-program) says only L&I-certified plumbers can legally do plumbing on someone else's property; owners can do their own. Check any firm on [Washington L&I Verify](https://secure.lni.wa.gov/verify/) before you sign.

## Why water heaters come up so often in Marysville

Marysville has grown fast, and its housing reflects that: older homes near downtown and along the State Avenue corridor, ramblers and split-levels from the 1970s and 1980s, and large newer subdivisions in areas like Sunnyside, Getchell, and Smokey Point. A tank water heater does not last forever, so every one of those eras eventually reaches the same moment — a leak at the base, rusty water, or a pilot that will not stay lit — usually on a weekday morning.

That urgency is exactly when homeowners skip steps. A replacement looks like a one-for-one swap, but it touches water piping, a pressure-relief discharge line, sometimes gas piping and venting, sometimes electrical, and the anchoring that keeps a full tank from tipping in an earthquake. The Board of Project Stewardship's [Marysville directory](../marysville.html), [plumber page](../plumber.html), [plumbing guide](../plumbing.html), and [permits overview](../permits.html) cover the broader picture. This guide sticks to what the City of Marysville and Washington L&I actually publish.

## The City plumbing permit

Marysville adopts the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code by ordinance. [MMC 16.08.010](https://ecode360.com/49264480) says it regulates "the installation, removal, alteration or repair of plumbing and drainage systems and fixtures and water heating and treating equipment." The City's amended permit fee table in the same chapter lists separate unit items for each water heater and/or vent, for gas piping systems, and for alterations of water piping. In plain terms: replacing the tank, its vent, or the gas line to it are all things the City expects to see on a plumbing permit.

The City's [exempted work list](https://marysvillewa.gov/DocumentCenter/View/9063/Exempted-Work-2021-IBC) is about structures — small sheds, low fences, short retaining walls, low decks, painting — and it ends with a reminder that "separate plumbing, electrical, and mechanical permits may be required" even for exempted items. A water heater is not on the exemption list.

How the process works, from the City's own pages:

- **Everything is online.** [Permit Services](https://www.marysvillewa.gov/1440/Permit-Services) says Community Development runs a fully online portal for permits and land use applications, with real-time status. The [Permit Portal](https://permits.marysvillewa.gov/) lists residential plumbing, mechanical, and electrical permit types.
- **Inspections are requested in the portal.** The portal says inspection requests must be submitted online by 3:30 p.m. to be scheduled for the next business day.
- **Questions go to a Permit Tech.** The City lists MSVLPermits@marysvillewa.gov and 360-363-8223 for portal help, and Building Services can be reached at 360-363-8100 to confirm whether a permit is required.
- **Watch for payment scams.** The portal carries a fraud alert: the City will never request payment by wire transfer.

## The electrical permit comes from the City, not L&I

In much of Snohomish County, electrical permits come from Washington L&I. Marysville is different. L&I's [City Electrical Permits & Inspections](https://lni.wa.gov/licensing-permits/electrical/electrical-permits-fees-and-inspections/city-electrical-permits-inspections) page lists Marysville among the cities that issue their own electrical permits, at 80 Columbia Avenue (360-363-8231), and the City's [Building Services](https://www.marysvillewa.gov/97/Building-services) page describes its electrical permit processing. The same L&I page links an address lookup, which is worth using because some Marysville mailing addresses sit outside city limits in unincorporated Snohomish County.

Why this matters for a water heater:

- A straight electric-for-electric swap may reuse the existing circuit, but the connection still has to be done to code.
- A switch to a **heat pump water heater** can change the circuit, the location, or the need for a condensate drain. [ENERGY STAR's heat pump water heater page](https://www.energystar.gov/products/heat_pump_water_heaters) is a neutral place to compare the product category before you talk to installers.
- A move from gas to electric, or electric to gas, changes the permit picture on both sides — electrical work on one end, gas piping and venting on the other.

Ask every bidder which permits they will pull and from which office. "We'll take care of it" is not an answer you can check.

## Who is allowed to do the plumbing

Washington's plumber certification law, [RCW 18.106](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=18.106.150), does not require a license for plumbing work on your own residence or other property you own, and it lets a householder get unpaid help from a friend, neighbor, or relative who is not in the plumbing trade. L&I's [About the Plumber Program](https://lni.wa.gov/licensing-permits/plumbing/about-the-plumber-program) page says it plainly: only L&I-certified plumbers can legally perform plumbing work on someone else's property, and property owners can perform their own plumbing on properties they own. L&I's [plumber certification page](https://lni.wa.gov/licensing-permits/plumbing/plumber-certification/) lists water heaters among the plumbing systems the certification covers.

Two things follow:

1. **If you pay someone, they need to be a certified plumber** working for a registered contractor. A handyman who "does water heaters" is not automatically qualified.
2. **Doing it yourself does not skip the permit.** The owner exemption is about who may do the work, not about whether the City inspects it.

## What to write into a Marysville water heater bid

A one-line quote ("replace 50-gal tank") leaves too much open. Ask for these in writing:

- **Permits:** which permits are included (plumbing, plus electrical or gas piping if the scope calls for it), who applies, and that the final inspection is part of the job.
- **The unit:** fuel type, tank or tankless or heat pump, capacity, and the model, so you can look up its warranty and requirements yourself.
- **Connections:** whether supply lines are being reused, replaced, or rerouted; shutoff valve condition; and whether an expansion tank is needed for your system.
- **Safety items:** the temperature and pressure relief valve and its discharge line, seismic strapping, and — for gas units — venting and combustion air.
- **Location changes:** if the heater is moving, the drain pan, floor drain, or condensate route, and any wall or floor that has to be opened.
- **Haul-away and cleanup:** removal and disposal of the old tank.
- **Warranty:** what the installer warrants on labor, separate from the manufacturer's tank warranty.

## How to shortlist and verify a plumber

Get two or three written bids built on the list above. Then:

- Look up each company on [Washington L&I Verify](https://secure.lni.wa.gov/verify/) — contractor registration, bond, and insurance — and confirm the person doing the work holds a plumber certificate.
- Ask who pulls the Marysville plumbing permit in the [Permit Portal](https://permits.marysvillewa.gov/), and, if electrical work is involved, who pulls the City electrical permit.
- Use the portal's contractor search and permit search to see the permit once it is issued.
- Walk through the Board's [verify a contractor](../verify-contractor.html) checklist before you sign.

## Next steps for a Marysville water heater replacement

1. Confirm your address is inside Marysville city limits using the lookup linked from L&I's [city electrical permits page](https://lni.wa.gov/licensing-permits/electrical/electrical-permits-fees-and-inspections/city-electrical-permits-inspections).
2. Decide whether you are replacing like-for-like or changing fuel or type, since that changes the permits.
3. Get written bids that name the permits, the unit, the connections, and the safety items.
4. Verify every firm on [L&I Verify](https://secure.lni.wa.gov/verify/) and confirm a certified plumber will do the work.
5. Make sure the plumbing permit — and any electrical permit — is issued through the City's [Permit Portal](https://permits.marysvillewa.gov/) before work starts.
6. Request inspections in the portal by 3:30 p.m. for the next business day, and do not skip the final.

A water heater is one of the most ordinary replacements in a house, which is why it is easy to rush. In Marysville, the permit, the right office for the electrical work, and a certified plumber are the difference between a swap that simply works and one you have to explain when you sell.
