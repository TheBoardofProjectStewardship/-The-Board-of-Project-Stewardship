How a house is built

Foundation types

Slab, crawl space, basement, and piers solve different ground, frost, and access problems. This house is drawn as a slab-level pad.

A slab on grade pours the floor on a prepared base, with a thickened edge or a stem wall and footing. A crawl space lifts the floor on short walls so someone can reach pipes and ducts. A basement is that wall, tall enough to be a room, and it has to resist soil and water. Piers and grade beams carry the house on points when the soil near the surface is poor or the site is steep.

The choice follows the soil, the slope, frost depth, and whether anyone needs to get under the floor. This sketch is a low pad with the main floor at grade. It is not a soils recommendation and not a basement.
