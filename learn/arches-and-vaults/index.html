How a house is built

Arches and vaults

An arch works in compression and pushes outward. This wood house uses bending members instead.

An arch carries load by compression along its curve. The curve wants to flatten, so each support is pushed outward. Those thrusts need heavy abutments or a tie across the opening. A vault is that idea extended into a roof. Masonry arches and vaults are old, and they still explain why a curved structure cannot simply sit on a thin wall.

This house does not use an arch. Its floors and roof are beams, joists, rafters, or trusses, which work in bending and deliver mostly vertical load when they are tied. The comparison is here so the wood frame has a contrast, not because the model is hiding a vault.
