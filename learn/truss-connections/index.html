How a house is built

The plate at a joint

The plate or gusset at a truss joint transfers the force. The joint is not a place to cut or drill.

Members of a truss meet at a joint. A metal plate pressed into the wood, or a nailed plywood gusset on site-built work, transfers the force from one member to the next. The plate size and the tooth pattern are part of the truss engineering. The lumber face alone is not the connection.

Do not cut, notch, or drill the plate or the member at that joint to run a duct or a wire. If a service has to pass, it passes through an opening the truss design already allows. This page shows the joint. It is not a plate specification.
