      1.Discovery

      Discovery

      Before drawings: sit at the kitchen table and capture vision, budget reality, coastal site notes, and proposal readiness.

      Board checklist (educational)

      • Client vision, use cases, and non-negotiables captured in discovery notes
      • Budget reality check + timeline / life events discussed (not a bid)
      • Edmonds / coastal site specifics flagged (drainage, setbacks, neighbors)
      • Next-step proposal readiness: photos, sketch, and open questions logged

      Coastal diligence

      • › Flag shoreline, steep slope, wetland, stream, and CARA overlays on Edmonds GIS before anyone designs stormwater.
      • › Ask how the homeowner will live through construction on a tight coastal lot — staging and neighbor courtesy start here.

      Ask your GC

      • What decisions should be written down before anyone draws?
      • Which coastal site constraints are already known?
      • Who owns the next proposal step?

      Gate: Vision, budget reality, and site notes are captured before plans start.

      2.Plans

      Plans

      Layouts and elevations turn discovery into a shared spatial story you can walk through on paper.

      Board checklist (educational)

      • Floor plans & elevations reviewed against the homeowner brief
      • Material submittals & finish direction started (Board template habit)
      • Long-lead items flagged early so procurement does not stall framing
      • Clarify: these are educational illustrations, not AHJ-approved documents

      Coastal diligence

      • › Confirm jurisdiction on Edmonds GIS (city limits vs county) before plan assumptions.
      • › Give published design criteria (snow, seismic, wind exposure) to the engineer of record — do not design from marketing copy.

      Ask your GC

      • Do these sheets match the brief we agreed?
      • Which long-lead items are already flagged?
      • Are we clear these are not permit sets yet?

      Gate: Layouts reviewed against the brief; educational sheets only — not AHJ-approved.

      3.Permits

      Permits

      Permits posted, contractor license verified, and site logistics set before dirt moves.

      Board checklist (educational)

      • Permits approved & posted at the job site
      • Contractor license re-checked at WA L&I Verify before deposits
      • Neighbor notification / right-of-way staging plan when the street is affected
      • Erosion-control / SWPPP plan active for coastal Puget Sound lots

      Coastal diligence

      • › City of Edmonds building applications run through MyBuildingPermit — not Snohomish County PDS for city-limits parcels.
      • › Do not quote review days or permit fees from memory; pull live queue and invoices from the portal / Permit Coordinators.

      Ask your GC

      • Are permits posted on site?
      • Was the contractor re-checked on WA L&I Verify?
      • Who owns neighbor / ROW notices?

      Gate: Permits posted and license habit confirmed before dirt moves.

      4.Site prep

      Site prep

      Clearing, access, erosion control, and staging so the crew can work clean and keep sediment off the street.

      Board checklist (educational)

      • Tree / vegetation clearing limited to the approved envelope; salvage or haul plan set
      • Construction entrance, fencing, and material staging laid out without blocking egress
      • Silt fence, inlet protection, and SWPPP measures in place and photographed
      • Temporary utilities / Porta-John / dumpster placed; site safety briefing logged

      Coastal diligence

      • › Edmonds: temporary erosion and sedimentation controls stay the contractor/owner’s responsibility through the job — plans and inspection card on site.
      • › When a project triggers state Construction Stormwater coverage, a SWPPP and BMPs apply from first soil disturbance through final stabilization (verify trigger with Ecology / your AHJ).

      Ask your GC

      • Are silt fence and inlet protections in place?
      • Where is staging so egress stays clear?
      • Is the inspection card / portal path ready?

      Gate: Erosion controls and staging set before excavation advances.

      5.Foundation

      Foundation

      Excavation, underground utilities, and concrete that will carry every later stage.

      Board checklist (educational)

      • Property lines & setbacks verified; erosion control still holding after dig
      • Excavation to plan depth; underground plumbing / electrical routed and bedded
      • Forms, rebar, vapor barrier, and pour sequenced with required inspections
      • Waterproofing & backfill complete before framing mobilization

      Coastal diligence

      • › Coastal drainage and waterproofing details matter more than on inland lots — ask what the PE specified at the footing / stem wall.
      • › Second-story / addition work often needs an existing-foundation evaluation by a WA PE — do not assume capacity.

      Ask your GC

      • Which inspections must pass before framing?
      • Are underground utilities bedded to plan?
      • Is waterproofing complete before backfill closes?

      Gate: Foundation inspections and waterproofing complete before framing mobilizes.

      6.Framing

      Framing

      Studs, joists, and roof structure rise from the foundation — the moment the house becomes a volume.

      Board checklist (educational)

      • Sill plates, floor system, and wall framing plumb & lined
      • Roof framing / decking and any structural steel set
      • Shear walls, hold-downs, and framing inspection readiness
      • Open 3D build and scrub — stud walls are the featured reveal

      Coastal diligence

      • › Seismic detailing (shear, hold-downs) is typical for Edmonds residential — confirm against the stamped set, not a generic checklist.
      • › Protect framing from coastal rain between sheathing and dry-in — temporary weather protection stays until dry-in holds.

      Ask your GC

      • Is framing inspection ready?
      • Are shear walls and hold-downs per the stamped set?
      • When does temporary weather protection go up?

      Gate: Framing plumb, lined, and ready for inspection before sheathing closes structure.

      7.Roofing

      Roofing

      Underlayment, flashings, and finished roofing dry the structure from above so interior trades are not racing rain.

      Board checklist (educational)

      • Roof sheathing fastened; drip edge and ice/water or underlayment continuous
      • Valleys, chimneys, walls, and penetrations flashed before finish roofing
      • Shingles, metal, or membrane installed to manufacturer and code path
      • Roof dry-in confirmed before trusting interior finishes to stay dry

      Coastal diligence

      • › WA Residential Code roof assemblies emphasize continuous underlayment and corrosion-resistant flashing at walls, penetrations, and slope changes.
      • › Do not assert city-wide ice-barrier rules from memory — confirm climatic design criteria on the adopted Table R301.2 for the parcel.

      Ask your GC

      • Is underlayment continuous at valleys and penetrations?
      • When is roof dry-in considered held?
      • Who verifies flashing before finish covering?

      Gate: Roof dry-in from above confirmed before moisture-sensitive interior work advances.

      8.Siding

      Siding

      Weather-resistive barrier, openings, and cladding that shed wind-driven rain.

      Board checklist (educational)

      • WRB / house wrap continuous; penetrations taped and photographed
      • Windows & exterior doors installed with verified pan flashing and head details
      • Rainscreen gap or drainage plane per detailing; starter and trim set true
      • Siding / cladding courses complete; openings sealed so WRB stays continuous behind trim

      Coastal diligence

      • › WA amendments require a water-resistive barrier and a means of draining water that enters the wall assembly to the exterior.
      • › On marine Climate Zone 4C lots, WRB continuity and window pan/head flashings are the durability controls — not cladding alone.

      Ask your GC

      • Is WRB continuous and photographed at penetrations?
      • Were window pan and head flashings verified?
      • When can temporary weather protection retire?

      Gate: WRB, openings, and cladding hold so the envelope sheds wind-driven rain.

      9.Plumbing

      Plumbing

      Supply, drain, and vent rough-in land in open walls and floors before insulation closes the cavities.

      Board checklist (educational)

      • Supply, DWV, and vent rough-ins complete to plan; nail plates at studs
      • Tub / shower valves and blocking set; stub-outs dimensioned for finish
      • Pressure / DWV tests witnessed as required by the jurisdiction
      • Plumbing rough inspection passed before insulation

      Coastal diligence

      • › MyBuildingPermit residential plumbing rough-in tip sheets call for DWV and supply tests, nail plates near framing faces, and shower liner details before cover.
      • › Ask which rough inspection must pass before insulation — covering untested piping is a classic rework mode.

      Ask your GC

      • Has the plumbing rough inspection passed?
      • Were pressure / DWV tests witnessed as required?
      • Are nail plates and blocking in place?

      Gate: Plumbing rough inspection passed before insulation.

      10.Electrical

      Electrical

      Branch circuits, panels, and device boxes land while walls are open — including low-voltage paths for data, security, and controls.

      Board checklist (educational)

      • Service / panel location set; branch circuits pulled and secured
      • Device boxes, makeup space, and AFCI / GFCI planning match the finish layout
      • Low-voltage pathways (data, AV, security, thermostat) roughed with high-voltage separation
      • Electrical rough inspection passed before insulation

      Coastal diligence

      • › WA electrical rules require work to stay visually accessible at inspection; request inspection before concealing or burying wiring.
      • › Low-voltage / telecom pathways are not “anything goes” when adopted TIA standards apply — plan data routes while walls are open.

      Ask your GC

      • Has electrical rough inspection been requested before cover?
      • Are AFCI / GFCI plans matching the finish layout?
      • Are low-voltage pathways roughed with separation?

      Gate: Electrical rough inspection passed before insulation.

      11.HVAC

      HVAC

      Ducts, equipment pads, refrigerant lines, and ventilation rough-in share the open-wall window with plumbing and electrical.

      Board checklist (educational)

      • Equipment locations, pads, and clearances confirmed; condensate path planned
      • Supply / return ducts or hydronic lines roughed; boots and chases firestopped as required
      • Fresh-air / bath / range ventilation paths set before drywall
      • Mechanical rough inspection (when required) passed before insulation

      Coastal diligence

      • › Coastal salt air and equipment location (garage vs exterior pad) affect durability — ask what the design assumed.
      • › Energy-path / duct testing notes stay educational — not a promised score.

      Ask your GC

      • Are equipment clearances and condensate paths set?
      • Is mechanical rough inspection required on this permit path?
      • Are ventilation paths set before drywall?

      Gate: Mechanical rough (when required) passed before insulation.

      12.Insulation

      Insulation

      Cavity fill and continuous weather-resistive discipline that matter on PNW coastal homes.

      Board checklist (educational)

      • Wall cavities cleaned; batts / spray foam at rim joists as designed
      • Exterior insulation / WRB continuity checked at penetrations after trade rough-ins
      • Insulation inspection passed prior to drywall hang
      • Energy-path notes aligned with WSEC-R credit planning when that path is used

      Coastal diligence

      • › WSEC-R calls for a continuous air barrier and insulation installation checks; dwelling-unit blower-door testing is part of the energy path when that path applies.
      • › Energy-path notes stay educational — never a promised score or rebate.

      Ask your GC

      • Have all MEP rough inspections cleared?
      • Was insulation inspection scheduled before hang?
      • Are rim joists and penetrations sealed as designed?

      Gate: Insulation inspection passed prior to drywall hang.

      13.Drywall

      Drywall

      Hang, tape, texture, primer, and paint turn the shell into rooms you can walk.

      Board checklist (educational)

      • Drywall hang, screw pattern, tape & mud complete
      • Texture & primer; scrap removed for a clean paint stage
      • Interior paint coats to the agreed finish schedule
      • Blower-door / energy testing when the chosen path requires it

      Coastal diligence

      • › Hold hang until insulation and MEP rough sign-offs are recorded — do not cover unsigned work.
      • › For wet rooms, ordinary gypsum behind tile is not automatically acceptable backing; follow membrane manufacturer and AHJ guidance.

      Ask your GC

      • Are cavities confirmed dry after weather delays?
      • Is the finish schedule locked before paint?
      • Does this energy path require blower-door testing?

      Gate: Hang, tape, texture, and paint complete to the agreed schedule.

      14.Cabinets

      Cabinets

      Kitchen and bath boxes, millwork, and tops — the first big homeowner-visible craft after paint.

      Board checklist (educational)

      • Field measure confirmed against shop drawings; wall blocking verified
      • Cabinets / millwork set plumb; fillers and scribe fit finished
      • Countertop template after boxes are final; sinks and cooktops coordinated
      • Hardware, soft-close, and appliance reveals adjusted before punch

      Coastal diligence

      • › Trade care standards call for millwork delivery into dry, broom-clean spaces with HVAC running and time to acclimate before install.
      • › Coastal humidity swings can rack doors — schedule final adjusts after the house is conditioned.

      Ask your GC

      • Was field measure confirmed against shop drawings?
      • Is wall blocking verified before boxes land?
      • Are allowances vs fixed packages in writing?

      Gate: Millwork set, leveled, and coordinated with tops before punch.

      15.Tile

      Tile

      Wet-area waterproofing, tile, and the handoff into broader flooring.

      Board checklist (educational)

      • Wet-area substrate and waterproofing membrane installed and flood-tested when required
      • Layout, slopes to drain, and movement joints agreed before thinset
      • Tile / stone set; grout and sealers cured per product
      • Transition to adjacent flooring planned so heights and underlayments match

      Coastal diligence

      • › Waterproof membranes must cover the backing under the tiled wet area; tile and grout alone are not a waterproofing system.
      • › Shower receptors need liner, slope, and weep details verified before finish tile proceeds.

      Ask your GC

      • Was wet-area waterproofing verified before tile?
      • Do slopes to drain match the agreed layout?
      • How do flooring transitions meet height?

      Gate: Wet-area membranes and tile complete; flooring handoff sequenced.

      16.Punch

      Punch

      Walk the nearly finished home with a tablet: gaps, touch-ups, and final inspections before keys.

      Board checklist (educational)

      • Owner + project steward punch walk with photo notes
      • Final building & trade inspections passed
      • Touch-ups, hinge / gap adjustments, and remaining items closed
      • As-built / closeout packet started for handoff

      Coastal diligence

      • › Final L&I electrical and city building finals both need to clear — do not treat one as covering the other.
      • › Exterior punch after a coastal rain cycle catches flashings and drainage misses indoor walks miss.

      Ask your GC

      • Have final building and trade inspections passed?
      • What remains on the photo punch list?
      • Is the closeout packet started?

      Gate: Punch items closed and finals passed before keys.

      17.Handoff

      Handoff

      Warranty contacts, maintenance habits, and a clear steward for questions after move-in.

      Board checklist (educational)

      • Warranty terms & emergency contacts delivered in writing
      • Systems orientation (HVAC filters, shutoffs, GFCI) completed
      • Named project steward for post-handoff questions
      • Keep L&I and permit records with the home file

      Coastal diligence

      • › Coastal maintenance habits (gutters, WRB penetrations, exterior caulk) belong in the handoff packet.
      • › Permit expiration / validity: ask the issued permit face — do not invent a generic months number.

      Ask your GC

      • Where are warranty contacts in writing?
      • Who is the named steward after move-in?
      • Are L&I and permit records with the home file?

      Gate: Warranty packet, orientation, and named steward delivered.

      Build Walkthrough FAQ

      Common questions about this educational tool from the Board of Project Stewardship.

      What is the Board of Project Stewardship Build Walkthrough?

      An educational, illustrative sequence from discovery to warranty handoff for Edmonds / coastal Puget Sound homeowners. It is not a bid, schedule, or stamped plan.

      Why are roofing and siding separate stages?

      Roof dry-in and wall weatherization are different inspection and diligence moments. Splitting them matches how a careful project manager walks a job.

      Why are plumbing, electrical, and HVAC separate?

      Each trade has its own rough inspection gate before insulation. HVAC stays visible on the rail so mechanical rough is not buried inside a single “MEP” chip.

      What do the optional Plumbing, Electrical, Roofing, and Siding layers do?

      They turn on illustrative overlays to help you notice those systems. Overlays are educational — not a site scan or BIM model.

      When should I re-check a contractor license?

      Re-check on WA L&I Verify before deposits. The Board of Project Stewardship does not issue licenses.

      Are the photos from a real Edmonds project?

      No. Stills and clips are illustrative media for teaching sequence — not photos of your job and not engineering documents.

      What happens at cabinets and tile if the still looks like an exterior?

      Those stages reuse a shared finish still for this draft while the checklist covers millwork and wet-area diligence. New art may follow later; honesty chips stay on.

      How does this relate to Site Visit and Final walkthrough?

      Discovery shares language with Site Visit & Discovery; Punch and Handoff pair with Final walkthrough and Good Steward reading on the Board Learn hub.
