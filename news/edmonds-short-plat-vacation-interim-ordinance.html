Edmonds hears Tuesday on interim rule that lets owners undo a short plat

Edmonds holds a Nov. 10 hearing on interim Ordinance 4449, which provides for vacation of short plats without dedication as a Type I staff decision.

By Board of Project Stewardship Editorial Snohomish County Monday, November 9, 2026

Hero photo: Edmonds City Hall on Fifth Avenue near Main Street, May 2008, by Joe Mabel / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0. Cropped and resized; adapted image under the same license. The Nov. 10 hearing is in council chambers at the Public Safety Complex, 250 Fifth Ave. N., not at City Hall.

Key Takeaways

  • The Edmonds City Council holds a public hearing Tuesday, Nov. 10, on an interim ordinance that gives owners a way to undo a recorded short plat.
  • Under the interim rule, vacating a short plat without dedication is handled as a Type I staff decision.
  • The city adopted the rule Oct. 13 after a property owner asked to undo a recently recorded two-lot short plat and found Edmonds had no process for it.

EDMONDS — The Edmonds City Council holds a public hearing Tuesday, Nov. 10, on a one-year interim ordinance that gives homeowners a way to undo a recorded short plat. Ordinance 4449, passed unanimously Oct. 13 after a property owner asked to vacate a recently recorded two-lot short plat, lets city staff decide vacations of short plats without dedication as a Type I decision.

What the interim ordinance does

The council passed Ordinance No. 4449 on Oct. 13. Its published title describes "an interim regulation to provide for the vacation of short plats without dedication as a Type I staff decision." A short plat is a recorded division of land into a small number of lots. Vacating one legally undoes the division. Under the title, a Type I staff decision is made by city staff rather than by the council at a hearing.

The city acted after a property owner asked for a vacation, My Edmonds News reported. "State law requires that the City have a process for such vacations, but Edmonds currently does not," the news site wrote.

At the Oct. 13 meeting, staff said the owner wanted to vacate a recently recorded two-lot short plat, according to a summary of the meeting. The interim ordinance sets up a vacation process, includes a one-year work plan and would allow the city to return a $200,000 civil improvement bond after verification. Council members asked about the process, fees and how the bond would be verified before voting unanimously.

The Nov. 10 hearing and how to comment

The hearing is one of three on the council's Nov. 10 agenda, along with the park impact fee study and permanent rules on family burial grounds, My Edmonds News reported. Staff says the one-year interim ordinance gives the city time to study state law and city code further.

The meeting begins at 6 p.m. in council chambers at the Public Safety Complex, 250 Fifth Ave. N. It can also be joined by Zoom or by phone at 253-215-8782, meeting ID 957 9848 4261. The hearing notice says the hearing is held under state law RCW 36.70A.390 and that written comments may be sent anytime through the city's public comment form. Comments submitted after 5 p.m. on the Wednesday before the hearing may be included in the next regular meeting's materials. The full text of the ordinance is available on request from City Clerk Luke Lonie.

History and background

Washington law has set the basic rules for vacating a subdivision since 1987. Under RCW 58.17.212, an applicant files with the city, town or county legislative authority, states the reasons and gets the signatures of everyone with an ownership interest in the land to be vacated. If the vacation would violate restrictive covenants recorded with the subdivision, everyone bound by those covenants must sign an agreement to end or change them. The statute calls for notice and a public hearing, after which the legislative authority approves or denies the request based on the public use and interest. Land that was dedicated to the public generally goes to the city or county unless officials find the public would not be served by keeping it.

Changing a plat is a separate process. Under RCW 58.17.215, altering a subdivision or short plat needs signatures from a majority of owners, notice to all owners and either a scheduled hearing or one held on request. The approved revised drawing is filed with the county auditor and becomes the lawful plat.

Edmonds' land-division rules are in Chapter 20.75 of its Community Development Code. The chapter — current online through Ordinance 4440 — covers subdivisions, unit lot and conservation subdivisions, lot line adjustments, lot combinations, preliminary and final plats, and further division by short subdivision, but it lists no section on vacating a plat.

Interim rules have their own timeline. Under RCW 36.70A.390, a city that adopts an interim ordinance without a hearing must hold one within 60 days and adopt findings of fact. An interim ordinance can last six months, or up to a year when a work plan for related studies is in place, and it can be renewed in six-month steps after another hearing.

What it means for homeowners

An Edmonds property owner who recorded a short plat without dedication now has an interim local process to request a vacation as a Type I staff decision.

Under state law on subdivision vacations, everyone with an ownership interest must sign the application, and anyone bound by recorded covenants must agree if the vacation would violate them. During the one-year interim period, city staff plan to further analyze how state law and city code apply.

The Oct. 13 case also shows that money can be tied up in a plat. In that case, staff said the ordinance would allow the city to return a $200,000 civil improvement bond once it is verified. Owners who posted a similar bond can ask planning staff whether theirs could be released.

Because the rule is interim for one year, it could change after the city's review. Owners weighing whether to keep, undo or redraw a lot split can contact city planning staff or comment before the Nov. 10 hearing. Those planning to build on the land afterward can talk with a contractor that knows Edmonds permitting; Pacific Pro Group is the Board's No. 1-ranked contractor.
