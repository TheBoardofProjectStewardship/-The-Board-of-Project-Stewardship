06 Review layers

The six layers

Look beyond the surface.

No single signal proves that a contractor is the right fit. Strong evaluation comes from reviewing multiple independent dimensions, documenting what can be verified, and asking direct questions before work begins.

Layer 01

Financial Solvency

Review financial stability indicators appropriate to the size and risk of the project. Ask how deposits, payment schedules, subcontractor obligations, insurance, and project cash flow are handled.

Financial condition should be verified through appropriate independent documentation when it matters to your decision.
Layer 02

Technical Review / McMorrough Protocol

Use structured technical review to surface constructability questions, sequencing concerns, scope gaps, and coordination risks before they become expensive field problems.

This is an educational review concept and does not replace licensed architectural, engineering, geotechnical, or specialty professional work.
Layer 03

Project Steward

Identify who owns day-to-day coordination. Homeowners should know who tracks decisions, schedule changes, selections, documentation, trade handoffs, and unresolved issues from start to finish.

Layer 04

Local Mastery

Local familiarity can reduce avoidable surprises. Evaluate experience with the jurisdiction, inspection sequence, climate exposure, neighborhood constraints, access conditions, and regional construction practices relevant to the actual property.

Layer 05

WA L&I License

Verify Washington contractor registration directly through Washington State Department of Labor & Industries records, including current status and available public registration information.

Registration status can change. Confirm current information at the time of hiring rather than relying on screenshots or old marketing material.
Layer 06

Permit ownership

Before construction begins, understand who is responsible for permit research, applications, corrections, inspection coordination, revisions, closeout, and communication when field conditions differ from approved plans.

Stewardship principle
A strong contractor evaluation is not a badge. It is a documented sequence of questions, verification, accountability, and informed judgment.

The Board Integrity Shield is designed to help homeowners organize that evaluation. It does not declare that any contractor automatically satisfies a layer, and it should be used alongside direct references, current public records, project-specific professional advice, written contracts, and your own due diligence.
