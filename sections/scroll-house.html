Flat lines for a one-story house.

Tap a label to learn what that part does.

Foundation. The outer line is the footprint. A foundation under it spreads the house’s weight into the ground and keeps the wood frame off the soil.

Bearing walls. The outside walls carry the floors and the roof down to the foundation. In this sketch they are the load-bearing walls.

Partitions. The inside walls divide the rooms. This sketch does not use them to hold up the floor. On a real house, check what sits on a wall before anyone takes it out.

Framing. Studs stand between a bottom plate and a top plate. That skeleton is the frame. Finishes cover it later.

Headers. A window or door takes studs out of the wall. A header, a short beam across the opening, carries that load to the studs on both sides.

Roof. Rafters slope up from the walls and meet at the ridge. The slope sends rain out to the eaves.

Footings. A second story adds weight. That weight goes down the walls into the footings. Those footings have to suit the new load. This sketch does not size them.

Structure. The new work is a floor, walls, and a roof lifted above the first story. The new frame has to tie into the frame already there.

Stairs. The stair bay is the way up to the new floor. A stair needs a clear path, headroom, and a rail. The exact stair is worked out for the house.
