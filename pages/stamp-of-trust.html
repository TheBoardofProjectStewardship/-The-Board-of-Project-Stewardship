Integrity Shield

Six layers of project stewardship

The Stamp of Trust rests on the Integrity Shield — six educational and editorial layers the Board of Project Stewardship uses to organize due diligence. Checkable signals, not a single marketing claim.

1

Financial Solvency

Licensing alone is not enough. The Board reviews bonding capacity against active project loads. Over-leveraged firms do not receive the Stamp of Trust — reducing homeowner exposure to liens and abandoned jobsites.

2

Technical Review / McMorrough Protocol

Portfolios are reviewed against architecture and construction reference standards using the McMorrough Protocol — an editorial lens for drawings, detailing, and completed work. Not a professional license or certification.

3

Project Steward

Every verified engagement expects a designated Project Steward — a single point of accountability who champions the homeowner’s vision against scope creep and budget drift. No salesman handoffs.

4

Local Mastery

Competence where the work happens: King County, Snohomish County, Seattle, Edmonds, and neighboring jurisdictions — including permitting paths, height limits, view corridors, and critical-area rules where they apply.

5

WA L&I License

Preference for firms with active Washington contractor registration. Homeowners should re-verify status at the official L&I Verify portal before hiring. The Board is not L&I.

6

Permit ownership

Clear answers on who pulls and owns each permit, who schedules inspections, and who closes corrections before cover-up. The Board of Project Stewardship does not issue permits.

Credential process

How to earn the Stamp of Trust

A multi-step path for contractors operating in the Pacific Northwest who want to signal Board-reviewed stewardship — not a one-click badge purchase.

Step 1

Prepare your file

Gather UBI, L&I contractor ID, insurance certificates, and three Seismic Category D framing case studies. Confirm a named Project Steward for active work.

Step 2

Submit application

Send the checklist package to editorial@boardofprojectstewardship.com with subject line “Stamp of Trust application.”

Step 3

Integrity Shield review

Editorial review across all six Integrity Shield layers, including technical review under the McMorrough Protocol and local jurisdictional evidence.

Step 4

Decision & badge grant

If approved, you receive permission to display the Official Stamp of Trust embed while your status remains current. Status can be withdrawn if records lapse.

FAQ

Common questions

Is the Stamp of Trust a government license? +

No. It is an independent editorial credential from the Board of Project Stewardship. Washington contractor registration is issued by L&I — always verify there.

Does the Board perform construction? +

No. The Board publishes construction standards and contractor directories. It does not perform construction under the Board name and does not sell contractor leads.

What is the Integrity Shield? +

Six stewardship layers: Financial Solvency; Technical Review / McMorrough Protocol; Project Steward; Local Mastery; WA L&I License; and Permit ownership.

Who owns the building permits? +

That must be explicit in your contract. The Board expects clear permit ownership and inspection accountability; the Board itself does not issue permits.

How do I re-verify a contractor’s L&I status? +

Use Washington Labor & Industries’ public contractor verification service at secure.lni.wa.gov/verify/. Because registration status can change, check it directly when making hiring decisions.

Can anyone buy placement on this page? +

No. The Stamp is awarded after review against Board criteria. Placement is not sold as advertising inventory on this credential page.
