The main floor, cut flat and seen from above.

Tap a label to learn what that part does.

Foundation. The outer line is this house and the garage. A foundation under it spreads the weight into the ground and keeps the wood frame off the soil.

Bearing walls. The outside walls of the house and the garage carry the floors and the roof down to the foundation. In this sketch they are the load-bearing walls.

Partitions. The inside walls divide the kitchen, living room, bedrooms, and the other rooms. This sketch does not use them to hold up the floor. On a real house, check what sits on a wall before anyone takes it out.

Framing. Studs stand between a bottom plate and a top plate. That skeleton is the frame. Lap siding and the board-and-batten gable cover it later.

Headers. The garage door and the windows take studs out of the wall. A header, a short beam across each opening, carries that load to the studs on both sides. This sketch does not size them.

Roof. The east face is a board-and-batten gable. A hip roof and a dormer cover the west wing, and the garage roof sits lower. The slopes send rain out to the eaves.

Porch. The porch is the covered landing by the court, with a rail. The patio under the upper floor stays open.

Balcony. The balcony is the open floor on the west side, beside the garage. It needs a rail.

Footings. The upper floor and the garage send weight down the walls into the footings. Those footings have to suit that load. This sketch does not size them.

Structure. The upper floor stacks on the main-floor walls: a floor, walls, and the roof above. It has to tie into the frame already there.

Stairs. The stair drawn on both floors runs across the plan and ends at the upper floor. A stair needs a clear path, headroom, and a rail. The exact stair is worked out for the house.

Teaching sketch. Not a structural design.

How the Board works

A repeatable filter: research the work, verify public signals, weight local mastery, then publish an editorial shortlist homeowners can use.

01

Research

Study how additions and remodels actually run across King County, Snohomish County, and Seattle — AHJ norms, coastal detailing, and stay-in-home vs vacate patterns.

02

Verify

Cross-check public WA L&I signals and third-party review aggregates. Prefer firms homeowners can re-verify themselves before a deposit.

03

Local mastery

Weight local height limits, critical areas, and coastal weather sequencing — including Edmonds Bowl rules where they apply — not generic statewide marketing claims.

04

Editorial shortlist

Publish ranked directories and field guides so homeowners can compare firms against standards — not paid placement.

Every ranking page points you back to WA L&I Verify before deposits or demolition.
